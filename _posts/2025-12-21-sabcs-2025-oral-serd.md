> Originally published on LinkedIn by [iSWT Community](https://www.linkedin.com/company/iswtc/) on December 21, 2025.

At the 2025 San Antonio Breast Cancer Symposium, Roche's oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) data was the biggest market-moving news. In the phase III lidERA trial (an adjuvant endocrine treatment study of over 4,100 patients with ER+/HER2–, stage I–III breast cancer), giredestrant reduced the risk of invasive disease recurrence or death by 30% versus standard endocrine therapy (invasive disease–free survival HR 0.70; 95% CI 0.57–0.87, p=0.0014, [source](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-12-10)). Roche's stock surged over 3.5% on the day of data release, building on a prior 6% jump following the topline announcement on November 18. The enthusiasm even spilled over to other SERD developers: Olema, which is developing a different oral SERD, palazestrant, saw its stock more than double intraday despite having no new pivotal data of its own.

The market reaction might seem surprising at first glance. This is a crowded field—so why are companies still entering, and why did the market respond so dramatically to the lidERA results?

The question is reasonable. ER+/HER2– breast cancer is the most common breast cancer subtype. As the nomenclature indicates, this subtype is primarily driven by the estrogen receptor (ER), making endocrine therapy the first-line backbone of treatment. For metastatic disease and selected high-risk early-stage patients, a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitor—which acts downstream of ER signaling—is added to the endocrine therapy. This combination is highly effective: 5-year survival exceeds 95% in the ER+/HER2- population as a whole (across mixed stages and treatment regimens). The clinician's toolbox is extensive, with multiple approved endocrine agents including SERDs, aromatase inhibitors (AIs), and selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs). The field is indeed crowded, but several key factors point to substantial room for innovation.

First, the total addressable market is enormous and durable. Breast cancer is common—a woman's lifetime risk in the U.S. is approximately 1 in 8. About 70% of newly diagnosed breast cancers are ER+/HER2-, making it the clear majority subtype. Patients typically remain on endocrine therapy for years, particularly in the adjuvant setting (post-surgery treatment to prevent recurrence). This extended treatment duration is why giredestrant's success in the adjuvant setting generated such significant market enthusiasm.

Second, unmet clinical needs remain substantial. Even with modern standard-of-care treatment, recurrence and resistance are common, creating clear value in better endocrine control. The lidERA trial provides the most compelling evidence: a 30% relative reduction in invasive disease recurrence or death versus standard endocrine therapy in early-stage disease represents a meaningful clinical advance.

Additional factors suggest continued opportunity in this space, demonstrated by other exciting data releases at SABCS 2025.

Setting and patient segmentation matter. The field has evolved into multiple distinct markets with different trial designs and competitive landscapes: premenopausal versus postmenopausal patients, early-stage versus metastatic disease, and biomarker-defined subgroups such as ESR1 mutations (rare in treatment-naïve disease but common in advanced/metastatic settings). The EMBER-3 trial continued building the commercial case for ESR1 mutations as a durable market segment. Lilly's imlunestrant (Inluriyo) is already FDA-approved for ER+/HER2–, ESR1-mutated advanced/metastatic breast cancer after progression on at least one line of endocrine therapy. SABCS 2025 updates, with median follow-up of approximately 28.5 months, revealed an overall survival (OS) benefit in the ESR1-mutant subgroup: median OS of 34.5 months versus 23.1 months for imlunestrant versus standard endocrine therapy (HR 0.60; nominal p=0.0043 in hierarchical testing). ([source](https://www.mskcc.org/clinical-updates/sabcs-2025-news-updated-ember-3-efficacy-results-for-imlunestrant-with-or-without-abemaciclib-in-er-her2-advanced-breast))

Finally, there is room for strategic innovation beyond new molecules. SABCS 2025 delivered the prespecified final PFS analysis of SERENA-6, advancing perhaps the field's most investable "endocrine-adjacent" concept: circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)-guided endocrine switching before radiographic progression. Patients receiving first-line AI plus a CDK4/6 inhibitor underwent serial ctDNA testing for emergent ESR1 mutations; upon ESR1 mutation detection (without radiographic progression), they switched from the AI to camizestrant while continuing the CDK4/6 inhibitor. In this final analysis, camizestrant switching significantly improved PFS (median 16.6 vs 9.2 months; HR 0.46; p<0.00001). SERENA-6 demonstrates that value can be added by optimizing the treatment paradigm: integrating monitoring cadence, companion diagnostics, and a drug that fits seamlessly into the first-line practice. This approach can extend time on endocrine-based therapy and potentially enables earlier introduction of next-generation endocrine therapy agents. ([source](https://sabcs.org/events/2025/rapid-fire-7/))

SABCS 2025 reinforced an enduring truth in oncology investing: even a "crowded" mechanism can be dramatically undervalued if someone proves it works in the right setting.
